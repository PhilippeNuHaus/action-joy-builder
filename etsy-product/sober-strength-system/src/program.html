Before You Begin

Stopping alcohol can be medically dangerous

If you drink heavily or daily, quitting suddenly without medical supervision can cause seizures, hallucinations, and delirium tremens, which can be fatal. Do not use a training program as a substitute for medical detox. Talk to a doctor, an addiction specialist, or call a helpline before you stop drinking. In the US, SAMHSA's National Helpline is 1-800-662-4357, free and confidential, 24/7.

This program is written for someone who is already past acute withdrawal — either because they have been cleared by a clinician, are working with a treatment program, or were not physically dependent. It is a training plan, not a treatment plan.

What this document is not

  • It is not medical advice and does not replace care from a doctor, therapist, or addiction professional.
  • It is not a recovery program. It does not replace mutual-aid groups, counselling, or medication-assisted treatment. It is designed to sit alongside them.
  • It is not a diagnosis tool. If drinking is harming your life, please speak to a professional.

Physical readiness

Get medical clearance before starting any resistance training program, especially if you have liver disease, heart conditions, high blood pressure, a history of seizures, are pregnant, or are taking medication that affects heart rate, balance, or blood pressure. Stop immediately and seek help if you experience chest pain, dizziness, fainting, or unusual shortness of breath.

The honest version

Lifting will not keep you sober by itself. Nothing in a PDF will. What good training does is give the hardest hours of your day a shape, give your body a reason to recover, and put something on the calendar that you can win. That is a real and useful thing. It is not the whole thing.

Part One — The Method

Why Your Training Was Never the Problem

You have probably trained before while drinking. You may have trained hard. And the results never quite matched the work. That was not a discipline failure. There was a physiological ceiling on top of everything you did.

24–38%
Reduction in muscle protein synthesis measured when alcohol is consumed after training, even with optimal protein intake.
REM
Sleep architecture is suppressed by alcohol. Deep and REM sleep are when the bulk of recovery adaptation happens.
~30d
Typical point at which sleep quality is reported to substantially recover after stopping.

The three levers alcohol was pulling

1. Protein synthesis. Resistance training creates the signal to build muscle. Alcohol blunts the response to that signal. Studies measuring myofibrillar protein synthesis after training found meaningful suppression even when protein was fed correctly. You were making the signal and then muting it.

2. Sleep. Alcohol is sedating, which is not the same as restorative. It gets you unconscious and then fragments the back half of the night, cutting into exactly the sleep stages that support recovery and hormonal function.

3. Recovery debt. Training damage plus alcohol processing plus poor sleep compounds. You showed up to the next session already behind, so you either trained fatigued or you skipped.

What this means for the next 12 weeks

You are about to train with the ceiling removed. That is genuinely exciting and it is also the reason this program starts easier than you want it to. In weeks 1 to 4 your recovery capacity is not yet restored — the alcohol is gone, but the sleep debt, the fatigue, and the nervous system disruption are still being paid down. Train for the body you have in week 2, and you get to keep training in week 10.

Figures above summarise published research on alcohol and post-exercise muscle protein synthesis and on typical early-sobriety recovery timelines. Individual response varies substantially. Sources are listed on page 30.

The 90-Day Recovery Curve

Your capacity to recover from training is not flat across these 90 days. It follows a rough, well-documented arc. Every phase of this program is matched to a segment of it.

WindowWhat is happening in your bodyWhat training must do
Days 1–28Sleep is fragmented as REM rebounds. Fatigue is often at its worst. Cravings peak early. Appetite and blood sugar are unstable.Build the habit. Low volume, never to failure. Frequency over intensity.
Days 29–56Sleep quality substantially improves for most people. Energy returns, though post-acute withdrawal waves can still hit without warning.Add volume. Introduce real progressive overload with an autoregulation rule.
Days 57–90Recovery capacity is broadly restored. Protein synthesis is no longer being suppressed. This is when visible change tends to arrive.Add intensity. Train heavier, prove the progress, test at week 12.

The wall around week 3

A very common pattern: the first week or two carries a burst of energy and optimism, then it drops out and you feel worse than when you started. This is expected and it is temporary. It is also precisely when most people abandon a training program. Phase 01 is deliberately easy enough to survive that week.

How This Program Is Built

Most programs assume your recovery is constant. This one assumes it is climbing, and loads you accordingly.

Reading the effort column

RPE means Rate of Perceived Exertion, scored by how many reps you had left in the tank. RPE 6 = four reps left. RPE 7 = three left. RPE 8 = two left. RPE 9 = one left. In Phase 01 you should finish every set feeling like you could have done several more. That is the instruction, not a lack of ambition.

The four rules

Rule 1 — The session you complete beats the session you planned

A shortened workout done on a bad day is worth more here than a perfect workout skipped. The streak is the asset.

Rule 2 — Never train to failure in Phase 01

Failure training costs recovery you do not currently have. It also makes the gym feel punishing, and you need it to feel like relief.

Rule 3 — Log the sober day number on every session

Every log page has a Day column. Filling it in is the point. By week 12 the training log and the sobriety record are the same document, and that document is difficult to argue with.

Rule 4 — When in doubt, check in before you decide

The Green / Amber / Red system on the next page decides what you do on hard days, so that a tired brain does not have to.

The Green / Amber / Red Check-In

Sixty seconds, every morning, before you look at the day's session. Score two things honestly. The score picks the workout, so you never have to negotiate with yourself in the evening.

Score thisAsk yourself
Last night's sleepSlept fine · Broken or short · Barely slept at all

Green

Slept fine and craving quiet.

Run the session exactly as written. Chase the prescribed load. This is a normal training day.

Amber

Either sleep was broken or the craving is noticeable.

Run the session, but drop the final set of every exercise and cap all sets at RPE 7. Keep the first exercise as written. Expect about 70% of a normal session.

Red

Barely slept, or the craving is loud.

Do the Red Session below instead. Do not skip and do not push. The ritual is the objective today, not the stimulus.

Mark it in the log as done, not as missed. This matters more than it sounds. If Red days count as failures, you will start lying on the check-in to avoid them, and the whole system stops working.

Training the Trigger Window

For most drinkers the danger is not spread evenly across the day. It clusters. Work ends, the house goes quiet, the evening opens up, and there is a shaped hole where a drink used to go. This program schedules training into that hole on purpose.

Find your window

Think back over the last month of drinking and answer plainly: what time did the first drink usually happen? For most people it lands somewhere between 5:00pm and 8:00pm, on the transition from working to not working. Write yours here:

My trigger window
to

Place the sessions

  • First choice: train inside the window. Start the session within thirty minutes of when the first drink used to happen. You are not just training, you are occupying the slot.
  • Second choice: train immediately before it, so that the window opens with you already showered, fed, and physically tired.
  • Morning trainers: if the gym has to be at 6am, that is fine — but you still need something in the evening window. Use Tier 1 or Tier 2 of the Substitution Ladder on the next page as a nightly anchor.

Why the gym bag goes in the car the night before

Every decision you have to make at 5:45pm is a decision that can go the other way. Pack the bag, book the class, set the alarm, tell someone you are going. Front-load every choice into a moment when you are not being asked to want it.

The evening does not need to be filled with something impressive. It needs to be filled with something scheduled.

The Substitution Ladder

For the moments when the craving arrives and training is not available — you are away, it is late, you have nothing with you. Work down the ladder until something fits. The rule is simple: complete one tier before you make any decision about drinking.

Tier 1
10 min
The Red Session, short form

Three rounds of 10 squats, 10 push-ups, 20-second plank. No equipment, no space required, no clothes to change into. Finish slightly out of breath.

Tier 2
5 min
Fifty and fifty

Fifty bodyweight squats and fifty push-ups, broken into as many sets as you need, in whatever you are wearing. Count out loud if you are alone. The counting occupies the part of your mind that is doing the negotiating.

Tier 3
2 min
Cold, breath, contact

Sixty seconds of cold water on your face, wrists, or a full cold shower. Then twenty slow breaths with a longer exhale than inhale. Then send one message to one person with your sober day number in it. You do not have to explain why.

Tier 4
30 sec
The door

Write your day number on the back of your hand. Put shoes on. Walk out of the front door and once around the block, or once around the building, or to the end of the corridor and back. Then reassess.

The ladder is a bridge, not a treatment

These are tools for getting through a moment. They are not a substitute for a sponsor, a therapist, a meeting, or a crisis line. If the craving is overwhelming or you are in danger, contact your support network or call SAMHSA's National Helpline on 1-800-662-4357 (US, free, 24/7). Put the numbers you would actually call in your phone today, before you need them.

Rest between sets

Phase 01: 60 to 90 seconds. Phase 02: 90 seconds on isolation, 2 minutes on compounds. Phase 03: 2 to 3 minutes on the heavy first exercise, 90 seconds elsewhere. If you are watching the clock and it is making the session stressful, stop watching the clock and rest until your breathing is normal.

How to add weight

When you hit the top of the prescribed rep range on every set of an exercise at the prescribed RPE, add weight next time. Smallest increment available: 5lb / 2.5kg on upper body, 10lb / 5kg on lower body. If you do not hit the top of the range, repeat the same weight. That is the whole progression model. It does not need to be more complicated than that.

Shift the days to fit your life, but keep at least one day between sessions, and put each session inside or just before your trigger window.

The walks are part of the program

Twenty minutes of easy walking on off days is not filler. It supports sleep, it burns off restless evening energy, and it gives the non-training days a shape too. Outside is better than a treadmill. Alone is fine.

What success looks like at day 28

You have completed ten or more of the twelve sessions, including at least one Red Session that you counted as a win. You are lifting weights that feel comfortably manageable. You have not missed two sessions in a row. You may not look any different yet. That is not what this phase was for.

If you relapse during this phase

Go to the next scheduled session. Not the perfect one, not after a fresh start on Monday — the next one on the calendar. Reset the day counter in the log and keep the training streak running. The training does not have to start over just because the count did.

The PAWS protocol

Post-acute withdrawal does not arrive on schedule. You can have a good fortnight and then get flattened for three or four days by low mood, broken sleep, and cravings that feel like week one again. This is documented, common, and temporary. It is also the single most likely reason a Phase 02 lifter stops training.

When a PAWS wave hits

Do not deload the whole phase. Deload the week. For as long as the wave lasts, run every session at Amber rules: drop the last set of every exercise, cap everything at RPE 7, keep the first exercise as written. If two consecutive days score Red on the morning check-in, take the rest of that week at Red Sessions only, then pick the program back up exactly where you left it. Do not restart the phase and do not try to make up the missed volume.

What success looks like at day 56

You have added weight to at least three of the main lifts since week 5. You survived at least one bad week without stopping. You are sleeping better than you were on day 1. Clothes may be starting to fit differently. Fourteen or more of the sixteen sessions are done.

What changes in this phase

  • Heavier top sets. The first exercise of each session now runs in the 3 to 6 rep range at RPE 8, with longer rests of 2 to 3 minutes.
  • Back-off sets. After the heavy top sets you drop roughly 15% and do a higher-rep set. This keeps the muscle-building volume while letting you train heavy.
  • The check-in still governs. Green, Amber and Red do not stop applying because you feel good. Ninety days in, an Amber day is still an Amber day.

A note on the ninety-day mark

Day 90 gets a lot of cultural weight, and some people find the days just after it strangely flat — the milestone arrives, nothing dramatic happens, and the structure that got you there suddenly has no obvious next target. Page 30 deals with this directly. Read it before you get to day 88, not after.

Phase 03 · Week 12

Test Week

The last week of the program is not another training week. It is the receipt. Four sessions, each one built around finding a heavy set of three to five reps on one main lift, recorded next to the day number you reached.

How to test a lift

  1. Warm up thoroughly: five to eight reps at roughly 50%, three at 70%, one at 85%.
  2. Take a set of five at what you believe is a hard but achievable weight.
  3. If it moved well and you had two reps left, add 5 to 10% and take one more set of three.
  4. Stop there. Two heavy sets is a test. Four is a competition and you are not in one.
  5. Never test a squat, bench, or deadlift alone without safety pins or a spotter.

Eating for a Body in Early Recovery

This is not a diet plan and it deliberately does not include one. Restriction in the first 90 days is a bad trade. Here are the few things that actually matter.

The four that matter

PriorityThe targetWhy it matters here
Eat enoughMaintenance calories at minimum through Phase 01 and 02.Under-eating raises irritability, worsens sleep, and makes cravings louder. Do not diet and quit drinking in the same month.
ProteinRoughly 0.7 to 1g per pound of goal bodyweight, spread across three or four meals.This is the input that the newly un-suppressed protein synthesis actually uses. It is the highest-leverage nutrition change you can make right now.
CarbohydrateDo not cut them, especially around training.Blood sugar is already unstable. Low-carb plus early sobriety is a recipe for the 4pm crash that sends people to the fridge or worse.
Fluid and saltDrink to thirst plus a bit more. Do not fear salt unless a doctor told you to.Hydration and electrolyte balance are often off in the first weeks and it shows up as headaches and fatigue.

About the sugar cravings

Wanting sweets in the first weeks without alcohol is extremely common and it is not a character flaw. Alcohol is a large, fast source of calories and reward, and its removal leaves a gap the body tries to fill quickly. Let it, within reason, for the first four weeks. Fighting sugar and alcohol at the same time means fighting on two fronts with one supply line. Deal with sugar in month four if it still needs dealing with.

One thing worth asking a doctor about

Heavy drinking is associated with deficiencies in several nutrients, including thiamine (vitamin B1), folate, and magnesium. Some of these matter a great deal and thiamine deficiency in particular can be serious. Do not self-prescribe from a supplement aisle — ask a doctor whether bloodwork or supplementation makes sense for you. This is a question for a clinician, not for a training PDF.

Day 91 and the Road After

The program ends. The thing it was built to support does not. Here is what to do with the week after test week, so that finishing does not feel like stopping.

Pick your next twelve weeks before day 90 arrives

  • Run it again, heavier. Start Phase 02 from your test week numbers and repeat Phases 02 and 03. This is the simplest option and it works for a long time.
  • Go to a dedicated strength or hypertrophy program. You now have twelve weeks of training history and honest numbers. Most intermediate programs will accept you.
  • Add something with other people in it. A class, a club, a lifting partner, a running group. The training has been solo by design so it could not be derailed by scheduling. That was for the first 90 days, not forever.

Keep the check-in

Whatever program comes next, keep the Green / Amber / Red morning check-in and keep logging the day number. They cost nothing, they outlast this document, and they are the two habits most worth carrying forward.

You did not just get stronger over these twelve weeks. You built a piece of evidence that the evenings can be filled with something else. Keep the evidence somewhere you can find it.
