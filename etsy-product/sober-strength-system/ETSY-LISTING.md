## 3. Description

The first two lines are what shows above the fold and what Google indexes. Everything

```
A 12-week strength program built for the first 90 days without alcohol — periodized to
the recovery your body is actually doing, not the recovery a normal gym program assumes.

Most workout plans assume your sleep and energy are stable. In early sobriety they are
not, and that mismatch is the reason so many people quit the program and the sobriety in
the same week. This one is built the other way round.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

WHY THIS IS DIFFERENT

Alcohol suppresses the muscle-building response to training — published research measures
reductions in muscle protein synthesis of roughly 24–38% when alcohol is consumed after a
session, even with perfect protein intake. So when you stop drinking, a ceiling comes off.

But it does not come off immediately. The first four weeks are when sleep is most broken
and fatigue is worst. Weeks five to eight are when sleep starts returning. By weeks nine
to twelve your recovery capacity is broadly restored.

This program has three phases matched to those three windows:

▸ PHASE 01 — THE ANCHOR (weeks 1–4)
  3 full-body sessions a week, deliberately low volume, never to failure.
  The goal is attendance, not muscle. It is easy enough to survive a bad week.

▸ PHASE 02 — THE BUILD (weeks 5–8)
  4 sessions a week, upper/lower split, volume climbs, real progressive overload
  begins. Includes a protocol for what to do when a post-acute withdrawal wave hits.

▸ PHASE 03 — THE CLAIM (weeks 9–12)
  4 sessions a week, heavy top sets and back-off sets. Ends with a test week
  so you finish holding proof of what changed.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

have to negotiate with yourself at 6pm.

★ TRAINING THE TRIGGER WINDOW
Your sessions get scheduled into the hours when you used to drink, on purpose. There is a
worksheet for finding your own window and placing training inside it.

★ THE SUBSTITUTION LADDER

Every training log also carries a sober-day column. By week 12 your workout log and your
```

## 4. Pricing

**Recommendation: list at `$22.00`, open with a 30% launch sale so it shows `$15.40`.**

Reasoning:

| Comparable | Typical Etsy price | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Generic printable sobriety tracker | $3–8 | One page of day-counting squares |
| Generic printable workout plan | $4–15 | Exercise list, no periodization |
| **This** | **$15–22** | 30-page periodized program + formula-driven tracker + support sheet |

The reason this can price above both categories is that it is not competing with either —
it is the only listing that serves both jobs at once, and neither commodity category
carries a 12-week structure, autoregulation rules or a spreadsheet with live formulas.

**Do not launch below $12.** In this niche a low price reads as another recycled PLR
tracker, which is exactly the association the product is built to escape.

Sequence:
1. **Weeks 1–4:** $22.00 list, 30% sale → $15.40. The sale badge drives early clicks and
   the first reviews, which are the real bottleneck on a new listing.
2. **After ~10 reviews:** end the sale, hold at $22.00, and watch conversion. If it holds,
   test $26.00.
3. **Later:** build a bundle at $34–39 — this plus a nutrition companion or a printed-and-
   bound version. Bundles lift average order value far more than raising the base price.

## 5. Listing images

Etsy gives you 10 slots. The first is ~85% of the click decision. Shot list in order:

1. **Main (2000×2000)** — the cover, with the words *12-Week Program · Built for the first
   90 days alcohol-free* clearly legible as a thumbnail on a phone.
2. **The differentiator** — the 90-day recovery curve from page 5. This is the single image
   that explains why the product exists.
3. **What's included** — a flat-lay grid of all 30 pages, small, showing volume for money.
4. **The Green / Amber / Red system** — page 7. Concrete, immediately understandable.
5. **A real workout page** — page 12 or 17, so buyers can see the actual prescription
   quality before purchasing.
6. **The tracker** — a screenshot of the spreadsheet dashboard with sample data filled in.
7. **The substitution index** — page 29, proving it works with minimal equipment.
8. **Text slide** — "Works in a full gym or a spare room with one pair of dumbbells."
9. **Text slide** — the what-you-get file list.
10. **Video (optional but worth it)** — a 10-second scroll through the PDF. Listings with
    video convert measurably better.

`make_listing_images.py` in `src/` generates 1, 2, 4 and 9 at 2000×2000 automatically.

## 7. Two compliance notes

1. **Health claims.** Keep the language exactly as written above. Describing the product as
   treating, curing or preventing alcohol use disorder would be both false and a policy
   problem. Everything here is framed as training programming, which is what it is. The
   research figures cited are about muscle protein synthesis, not about recovery outcomes,
   and they should stay that way.

2. **The safety notice is not optional.** Withdrawal can be genuinely dangerous. Keeping the
   medical-clearance line and the helpline number in both the listing and the files is the
   right thing to do, and it also protects you.
